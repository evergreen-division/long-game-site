Twelve years, one company, one city

  1. 2014

    A letter in the mail

    At 18, a checker at Safeway, he got a letter from Vector Marketing and threw it away. His mom kept it. Three weeks later he was fired from Safeway, and the letter came in handy. He sold $1,100 on his first three appointments, nothing the next day, and tried to quit 72 hours after training. He stayed, and sold just under $80,000 in his first ten months.

    Published · Open Letter, 2025
  2. 2015

    Into management, under his first mentor

    Nine months in, he was promoted to Pilot Sales Manager at the Washington Division headquarters in Seattle, under Ryan Casey, who he calls "my first and most impactful mentor." He says he was the youngest person ever promoted to that job.

    Published + recorded call
  3. 2016

    The couch years

    He paid a friend to sleep on his couch, then lived in his aunt and uncle's spare room, then a sorority house. He sold his racing bike for a fifth of its value to pay for gas and food. "I never counted the hours."

    Published · Floors & Ceilings, 2023
  4. 2017

    His own office, above a karate studio

    At 22 he signed a $27,000 commercial lease on Bel-Red Road in Bellevue and made a 10-year commitment to build something there. His first summer he sold under $100,000 against a $500,000 goal.

    Published · Open Letter, 2025
  5. 2018

    From $152,000 to $732,000

    District sales grew almost five times in a year, finishing as the #2 new office in the company. A year later his office had the second-highest sales productivity of hundreds of offices across the U.S.

    Published · 2019 and 2025 posts
  6. 2022

    #1 office in the company

    His district finished first in the company in the District Team competition and won the Silver Cup.

    Public · Changing Lives Selling Knives, ep. 382
  7. 2023

    Coaching other people's teams

Floors & Ceilings: $200,000 decisions

I recently had a phone call with an applicant who was weighing his options on taking a position with my company or taking a position with a furniture sales company. He was initially attracted to the furniture sales position because it had a slightly higher base pay ($22.50 base vs. $20.00 with me).

Base pay: the floor.
Commission and earning potential: the ceiling.

The first year I earned over $100,000, over 70% of it was performance-based. The first year I earned over $200,000, 83% was performance-based.

In 2014, 2015 and 2016, I trained and developed for the position I have now. I made less than $25,000 each year, working 45 to 55 hours a week year-round. I used to pay my buddy Mike a few hundred dollars a month to sleep on his couch…

When I opened my own location in 2017, I made a 10-year commitment to build something in Bellevue without the distraction of curiosity or job shopping.
